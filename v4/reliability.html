Library/Reliability & Operations/Reliability

Reliability · fundamentals to production judgment

Keep useful work recoverable when dependencies fail

Follow a checkout request through slow services, lost responses and process crashes. Derive deadlines, retries, idempotency, circuit breakers and recovery from those failures.

Technical definitionReliability is the ability to perform the required function correctly over time under stated conditions. Availability concerns whether the service can be used; recoverability concerns restoring correct operation and data after failure.
Before you begin. Understand an API contract, local transactions, and acknowledgement windows. We introduce each resilience mechanism before combining them.

Learning outcome: Assign retry and recovery ownership without confusing a timed-out request with a rolled-back business action.

1. A slow provider consumes the whole service

The earlier lessons made local state correct and pending work durable. A dependency can still be slow, unreachable or uncertain. Reliability asks whether the required business function remains correct and recoverable under those conditions. Availability of a process is only one part: an HTTP 202 with permanently stranded orders is not successful completion.

Follow one payment call. The provider can reject it, accept it and respond, or accept it while its response disappears. A timeout stops the caller’s wait without distinguishing those outcomes. Resource protection and business recovery therefore need different mechanisms, introduced below before combining them.

2. Follow an unknown outcome through recovery

Read this slowly: it establishes the distinction used in the implementation sections.

A timeout bounds waiting; it does not roll back a remote operation. A retry makes another attempt; it does not prove that repeating is safe. A circuit breaker limits calls to a failing dependency; a bulkhead isolates capacity. Learn these responsibilities separately before composing them.

Capture payment with operation ID pay_7. The provider acts, but the response is lost. Store or retain an UNKNOWN state and look up pay_7 using the provider contract. If another attempt is allowed, reuse that identity within the provider retention rules. Never create a fresh operation merely because a socket timed out. Meanwhile release local resources according to deadlines; recovery must survive beyond that HTTP request.

Check your understanding
FoundationalEvery failure should trigger three retries.
Only transient failures with safe repetition and remaining time/load budget should be retried. Layered retries multiply traffic. Validation errors, exhausted deadlines and ambiguous unsafe writes need different policies.
IntermediateWhy is a circuit breaker insufficient for a slow database?
Calls already in progress can consume the pool before it opens. Bound connection acquisition, statement execution and admitted concurrency. Measure cancellation behavior and lock/transaction cleanup.

3. Follow one operation before adding patterns

Carry this concrete operation through the later mechanisms.

A request sends capture to a payment provider. The provider may reject it, complete it, or complete it while the response disappears. A timeout distinguishes none of those by itself. The local application must both stop waiting within a budget and preserve enough identity to find the real outcome later. The first is a resource-control problem; the second is a business-correctness problem. Different mechanisms are needed for each.

4. Identify what must remain correct

Define eligible requests, acceptance and completion targets, and forbidden effects. State which work can be delayed and which facts must survive a crash. A payment operation may become pending/unknown without being declined; the caller needs a durable status path and an owner for reconciliation.

Include finite resource and recovery budgets. How long may requests wait, how many may wait together, how much retry traffic is allowed, and how old may unresolved operations become? These choices connect reliability to capacity. A policy that retries forever can preserve neither latency nor bounded cost.

Functional requirements

  • Accept checkout, expose payment status and reconcile uncertain attempts.
  • Keep status reads usable during provider problems.
  • Let operators recover failed/unknown work safely.

Targets and invariants

  • One capture per payment intent under the provider protocol.
  • Illustrative p95 status read below 200 ms.
  • Bound dependency concurrency and queue length.
  • Explicit RTO/RPO and durable intent retention.

An SLO is a measurable reliability target over a window. An invariant is a condition that must hold, such as not shipping an unpaid order. A fallback can preserve availability while violating an invariant if chosen carelessly. Serving a stale product description can be acceptable; fabricating payment success cannot.

5. First bound waiting time

Bound the resources consumed by one wait before considering another attempt.

A timeout usually bounds a particular wait according to a client/library’s semantics. A deadline bounds the useful lifetime of a logical operation across waits and attempts. Allocate remaining time for admission, connection acquisition, dependency work, local commit and response handling; do not give every nested call a fresh original budget.

Cancellation may stop local interest without undoing work already accepted elsewhere. Verify whether the underlying client releases connections/tasks, and preserve durable recovery for possible side effects. A shorter future timeout that leaves network work running can hide resource consumption instead of limiting it.

Example 800 ms total budget:
  validation/admission: 50 ms
  provider + any allowed retry: at most 500 ms
  persistence/response: 150 ms
  safety margin: 100 ms
Do not reset to a fresh 800 ms at every downstream hop.

Configure connect, pool-acquisition and response/read timeouts for the actual HTTP client. Cancellation may interrupt local waiting without stopping remote work. A timeout cannot roll back an already-committed provider action. Use durable state and status lookup to resolve it.

6. Retry an identity, not a new intent

Retry only after establishing safe repetition, a potentially transient failure and remaining time/load budget. Validation errors need corrected input, while an ambiguous unsafe write needs identity/inquiry rather than a new intention. Keep attempt identity separate from the stable business identity.

Backoff and jitter spread attempts; they do not make an unsafe effect safe. Multiple retry layers can multiply downstream calls, so choose an owner and count the initial attempt consistently. Reconciliation may continue durably after the original HTTP deadline, under its own bounded policy and unresolved-tail handling.

7. Add circuit breaking and bulkheads

Safe retries still add load. Isolate that load so one dependency cannot consume the service.

A circuit breaker can stop some future calls after observed failure behavior; a bulkhead limits how much shared capacity one dependency or workload can occupy. Neither automatically cancels calls already in flight. Bound connection acquisition, execution and admitted concurrency together so a slow provider cannot exhaust the service first.

Choose degradation from the operation. A stale catalog description may be permitted; a fabricated payment success is not. Separate critical and optional work, keep recovery/admin capacity, and avoid retry loops that overwhelm a recovering dependency. A breaker’s recovery probes must also fit the downstream budget.

MechanismMeaningWhat it does not do
Circuit breakerTemporarily stop calls after evidence of dependency trouble; probe recoveryIt does not make a command idempotent.
BulkheadLimit resources assigned to one dependency/work classIt does not increase downstream capacity.
Rate limiterBound arrivals over timeIt does not necessarily bound concurrent slow work.
Bounded queueLimit waiting work and expose overloadIt does not solve sustained excess demand.

Circuit breakers usually move among closed, open and half-open states. Half-open probes should be bounded; releasing every waiting caller when one probe succeeds can overwhelm a recovering dependency. Choose thresholds from real traffic, not arbitrary defaults.

Separate payment-call concurrency from status-read resources. Reject early with a documented response when a budget is exhausted. Keep fallback behavior visible in metrics; a service can report 200 while returning unusable substitute data.

8. Spring and AWS implementation boundaries

Configure HTTP timeouts in the actual underlying client, and compose resilience libraries according to their scope. Retrying inside versus outside a circuit breaker changes observed attempts and load; applying a time limit without resource cancellation can leave hidden work. Test the configured composition rather than assuming annotations are interchangeable.

For remote business actions, persist intent and stable provider identity before relying on the result. Use conditional state transitions and a durable worker/workflow where needed. Spring local transactions, SQS dispatch and a managed orchestrator can implement parts of the protocol; none chooses the business policy for late success, expiry or an unavailable inquiry endpoint.

// Teaching pseudocode: explicit semantic order, not a library API signature.
requireRemainingDeadline();
return boundedPaymentConcurrency.execute(() ->
    retryBudget.executeSafeAttempts(() ->
        breaker.call(() -> provider.capture(stableIntentId, request))));
// HTTP client timeouts and durable UNKNOWN-state recovery remain separate.

Use Spring transaction management for durable intent and outcome updates, with remote calls outside long-held DB transactions. RDS holds the ledger; SQS can schedule retry/reconciliation work; Step Functions can coordinate explicit states when its execution model fits. ALB/API Gateway limits and SDK retry policies must be included in the end-to-end budget.

A multi-AZ deployment addresses a failure domain; it does not protect against a bad release, corrupt writes or region-wide dependency failure. Test backup restoration and failover routing, and define who can initiate recovery.

9. Worked design: provider outage during checkout

Combine bounded waits, stable identity and a durable UNKNOWN state into a recovery protocol.

Payment intent pay_7 is submitted; the provider accepts it and the response is lost. Retain UNKNOWN and reconcile the original identity through the supported provider contract. A second fresh key would create a different intention and can charge again. If retention or inquiry constraints prevent establishing the outcome, surface that limitation and route the unresolved case under business policy.

While recovery runs, release finite request resources and expose the durable state. Test crashes around intent creation, provider acceptance and result recording; repeat callbacks and race cancellation/expiry with late success. Recovery is proven by a known correct business outcome or a clearly owned unresolved state, not by the circuit breaker closing.

10. Failure drills and recovery evidence

Failover and hedging can duplicate irreversible work if they create independent effects. Use them only under a protocol that preserves the required business identity and guarantee. Similarly, treating every HTTP 500 as retryable can amplify deterministic bugs or replay work whose result is unknown.

Observe useful completions, pending age, resource waits and recovery backlog alongside HTTP errors. A service can appear fast because it rejects everything or because it acknowledges work it never completes. Define the eligible population and success boundary so the reliability claim matches the customer journey.

DrillAssertion
Kill worker immediately after provider captureAttempt becomes resolvable with original identity.
Provider stays slow for an hourPools/queues stay bounded; status reads remain usable.
Duplicate success after refundConditional state/version policy prevents regression.
Breaker recovers at traffic peakProbe and release policy avoids a new surge.
Restore ledger backupMeasured RPO/RTO meet the declared recovery contract.

Track timeout rate, attempt count, retry amplification, breaker state, rejected concurrency, oldest unknown age and business discrepancy count. A green CPU chart is insufficient if payments remain unknown for hours.

11. Interview ladder

FoundationalTimeout versus failure?
A timeout means the caller did not receive a result in time. The remote side effect may have completed, so classify the outcome as unknown when appropriate.
FoundationalCircuit breaker versus retry?
Retry performs another attempt. A circuit breaker suppresses attempts when a dependency appears unhealthy and probes for recovery. Their composition must not amplify overload.
IntermediateWhy use both a rate limit and a concurrency cap?
A rate limit bounds arrivals per time; slow operations can still accumulate. A concurrency cap bounds simultaneous resource use.
IntermediateWhat makes a fallback safe?
Its result and side effects must preserve the operation’s business contract. Stale display data can be acceptable; invented payment success is not.
AdvancedWhat if the provider has no key or status inquiry?
Local coordination cannot prove a single remote effect across arbitrary lost-response windows. Require a stronger protocol or explicitly accept bounded retry/manual-settlement risk.
AdvancedHow do you prevent recovery from causing another outage?
Bound probes, retry/reconciliation rate, queue drains and concurrency. Keep resource headroom and separate critical paths, then test a prolonged outage and synchronized recovery.
IntermediateWhy can a shorter timeout still leave the service exhausted?
The caller may stop waiting while underlying network/task work continues to hold resources. Verify cancellation and cleanup, bound admitted concurrency and pool waits, and preserve recovery for already-accepted remote effects.
AdvancedCan recovery continue after the HTTP deadline?
Yes, through a durable intent and separately bounded recovery policy. The response deadline limits the caller wait; it must not erase the identity and evidence needed to resolve a possible committed effect.
