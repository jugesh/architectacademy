Digital principles

A digital signal represents values from a discrete set. In binary circuits, voltage ranges represent logic 0 and 1. Physical voltage still changes continuously. Valid input thresholds and timing let gates interpret signals reliably. Digital logic implements Boolean functions, and a digital computer combines logic, state and control to execute instructions over encoded data.

Boolean algebra and gates

Use + for OR, adjacency for AND, ¬ for NOT and ⊕ for XOR. XOR is 1 when its two inputs differ. NAND and NOR negate AND and OR respectively and are each functionally complete. For example, tying the inputs of a NAND together produces NOT.

Truth tables and Karnaugh maps

Number rows by their binary input with the leftmost variable as the most significant bit. For canonical sum of products (SOP), OR the minterms of rows where F=1. A minterm ANDs all variables, complementing a variable whose input is 0. For canonical product of sums (POS), AND the maxterms of rows where F=0. A maxterm ORs all variables, complementing a variable whose input is 1.

Copy each row’s F value into its matching map cell. Arrange two-bit axes as 00, 01, 11, 10, so neighbours differ in one bit. Group 1s for SOP or 0s for POS in rectangles of power-of-two size. Opposite edges wrap, overlaps are allowed and diagonals are separate. Include an X (don’t care) only where it improves a group. Cover every required cell. A variable that changes inside a group disappears. In SOP a fixed 1 gives the plain literal, and in POS a fixed 0 gives the plain literal.

Worked POS with two sum terms. For F(A,B,C) with zeros at 000, 001 and 010, group 000 with 001 to get (A+B), and group 000 with 010 to get (A+C). The minimized POS is (A+B)(A+C). Distributing and applying absorption gives the equivalent SOP A+BC.

Number systems and arithmetic

Base b uses digits 0 through b−1. Positions have weights b⁰, b¹, b² moving left from the units place and b⁻¹, b⁻² moving right of the point. Binary uses base 2, octal 8, decimal 10 and hexadecimal 16, with A through F representing 10 through 15.

  1. To decimal. (101.101)₂ = 4+1+½+⅛ = 5.625.
  2. From decimal. Repeatedly divide the integer part by the target base and read remainders in reverse. For 45 to binary the remainders are 1,0,1,1,0,1, giving 101101₂. Multiply a fractional part by the base repeatedly and read the integer parts in order. For .625 in base 2, these are 1,0,1. Some fractions repeat.
  3. Binary to octal or hex. Group bits from the radix point outward in threes or fours and pad the outer ends if needed. 101101₂ = 55₈ = 2D₁₆.

Carry when the column sum reaches the base. Borrow one unit of the next place, worth b units of the current place. In 57₈ + 26₈, 7+6=13 decimal gives digit 5 and carry 1, then 5+2+1 gives digit 0 and carry 1. Binary multiplication uses shifted partial products, so 101₂ × 11₂ = 1111₂. Binary long division uses compare, subtract and shift, so 1111₂ ÷ 11₂ = 101₂.

With n bits, two’s complement represents −2n−1 to 2n−1−1. To compute A−B, add A to the n-bit bitwise complement of B plus 1 and discard carry out. Signed addition overflows when equal-sign inputs produce an opposite-sign result. For example, four-bit 0111 + 0001 gives 1000, which is outside the signed sum’s representable range.

Binary codes

Parity and CRCParity detects any odd number of flips. A cyclic redundancy check uses polynomial division over GF(2) to detect error patterns determined by its generator.
Hamming codeParity checks locate single-bit errors. With r parity bits for m data bits, choose 2ʳ ≥ m+r+1 so each single-bit position and the no-error case have distinct syndromes.

Hamming(7,4) with even parity places parity at positions 1, 2 and 4, counting from the left here. Put data 1011 at 3, 5, 6 and 7. The parity groups are {1,3,5,7}, {2,3,6,7} and {4,5,6,7}. This gives 0110011. A flip at position 6 fails checks 2 and 4, so the syndrome is 6. Adding overall parity gives single-error correction and double-error detection (SECDED). In general, minimum distance d guarantees detection of up to d−1 errors or correction of up to ⌊(d−1)/2⌋ errors when used for that task.
