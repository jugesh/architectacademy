Consistency & Distributed Transactions · fundamentals to production judgment

State what readers may see and which changes must agree

Move from one transaction to replicated reads and multi-service workflows without confusing local atomicity, consistency models and business compensation.

Technical definitionA consistency model defines permitted observations of concurrent operations. A distributed transaction coordinates atomic commitment across participants; a saga instead coordinates local commits with explicit recovery and compensation semantics.
Before you begin. Read local transactions and the outbox. Keep a payment and inventory reservation as the running example.

Learning outcome: Choose a consistency guarantee per operation and explain intermediate states, partition behavior and recovery.

1. The order exists on one copy but not another

The order transaction committed, but a status read from another copy says not found. Keep the write outcome and the read observation separate: the writer may be correct and the replica delayed. The missing agreement is which observation the caller was promised after acceptance. Read-your-writes is a user-journey guarantee, not something implied by the existence of replicas.

Now let inventory and payment belong to independent services. A fresh inventory read does not make both services commit together. Reader freshness and multi-resource atomicity are different dimensions. We will first specify permitted observations, then explain coordination and the intermediate states it must recover.

2. Separate fresh reads from atomic workflows

Read this slowly: it establishes the distinction used in the implementation sections.

Consistency describes the observations and histories an operation permits. Transaction atomicity describes whether grouped changes commit together. You can atomically commit an order on the writer and still read an older value from a replica. Conversely, a fresh single-row read does not make a multi-service checkout atomic. State which property the user journey actually needs.

A customer pays and immediately opens order status. A stale view may briefly say pending, which could be acceptable if documented. Using that stale view to start another charge is a different decision with a stricter correctness requirement. Keep the authoritative payment transition in a controlled write path. Use a read model for display only when its delay does not authorize an unsafe action.

Check your understanding
FoundationalA saga is an ACID transaction spread over services.
A saga coordinates local commits and recovery actions. Intermediate states are visible and compensation can fail or be semantically incomplete. It requires durable progress, repeatable steps and reconciliation.
IntermediateCan a refund compensate every payment failure?
No. Refunds can fail, take time, incur fees or be forbidden in a particular state. Model irreversible effects and business recovery explicitly rather than treating compensation as a mathematical inverse.

3. Choose guarantees by operation

Choose guarantees per operation. Browsing and analytics may tolerate delayed copies; authorizing a financial transition may require authoritative current state. A customer’s immediate post-create lookup can need read-your-writes without requiring every reader worldwide to observe one real-time order. State the boundary rather than saying all data must be “strongly consistent.”

Also define what a write acknowledgement means under the store/topology and which failures the contract covers. A local durable commit, replicated acknowledgement and completion of a remote workflow are not interchangeable. Under lost communication, describe whether an operation waits, rejects, accepts pending work or permits a domain-defined conflict.

Functional requirements

  • Create order and immediately retrieve its accepted state.
  • Reserve inventory and authorize payment across services.
  • Expose workflow progress and reconcile partial completion.

Targets and invariants

  • Read-your-writes for the order-creation experience.
  • Never confirm fulfillment without its prerequisites.
  • Permit delayed analytics/search projections.
  • Define behavior when components cannot communicate.

Uniform “strong consistency everywhere” can impose cost without solving a business need. Define current-state reads for money decisions, session guarantees for a user experience, and bounded/documented lag for projections. State whether a write acknowledgement represents local durability or replicated commitment.

4. Explain the observations

A consistency model describes which histories of observations are allowed. Linearizability makes the specified operations appear to take effect at points between invocation and response while respecting completed real-time order. Read-your-writes promises a caller visibility of its own completed writes. Causal guarantees preserve causal relationships, while unrelated concurrent changes still need a conflict policy.

Eventual convergence alone gives no maximum staleness interval. Serializable transaction isolation is about equivalence to a serial execution, not automatically real-time ordering across clients. Apply these names to the actual operation and system boundary; a correct guarantee for one key or transaction cannot silently be extended to an entire multi-service purchase.

GuaranteeIntuitionNuance
LinearizabilityEach operation appears to take effect at one point between invocation and completionRequires the specified operation/system scope, not merely a replica count.
Read-your-writesA caller sees their own completed writesDoes not imply every caller sees one global order.
Causal consistencyCausally related operations are observed in causal orderConcurrent unrelated operations need additional conflict policy.
Eventual consistencyCopies converge if updates stop and the protocol progressesDoes not by itself specify maximum lag or conflict meaning.

Serializable isolation concerns equivalence to a serial transaction order; it does not automatically imply real-time ordering across clients. Name the guarantee and scope rather than using “consistent” as an unexplained adjective.

5. Reason from read histories

A consistency model restricts which observations a system may return. An isolation level restricts concurrent transactions. Their names are not interchangeable. A linearizable single-object register respects completed operations in real time; serializable transactions have an outcome equivalent to some serial transaction order, without that name alone promising real-time order. An application still has to choose the right object/transaction boundary for its invariant.

HistoryPromised modelAssessment
A write of version 8 completes; a later independent read returns 7Linearizable read of the same registerViolates the completed-write real-time requirement.
A session writes 8, then reads its own state as 7Read-your-writes for that sessionViolates the session promise even if other readers may lag.
A session reads 8, then reads 7 without an allowed resetMonotonic readsMoves backward in the promised session order.
Two transactions each use a consistent snapshot but jointly break a set-wide invariantSnapshot isolation aloneA stable snapshot is not proof of serializability.

Read-your-writes is scoped to a defined session/causal chain. Another device or a later request without the required session token may not carry that guarantee automatically. If the product promises that a user sees their order immediately from any device, that is a broader routing/authority requirement. Spell out identity, session lifetime and what happens if the freshness condition cannot be met.

Conceptual session freshness protocol:
  write -> acknowledged source position/version v
  next read carries a trusted minimum-observed position v
  selected replica must demonstrate it has applied that position
  otherwise wait within a deadline, route to an eligible authority,
  or report the defined freshness failure

A fixed sleep of 200 ms is not evidence that the replica applied v.
A row's business version may not cover every row used by the query.

Positions must be comparable under the actual replication contract. A log position from an old timeline or unrelated shard is not automatically meaningful after promotion or routing changes. An aggregate version can order that aggregate’s changes while saying nothing about a joined account balance. Keep the guarantee’s scope attached to the token instead of treating “version” as a universal freshness number.

Causal dependence also crosses operations. If a message says “payment completed,” a downstream read that cannot yet observe the associated committed fact may need a causal/freshness protocol or an event representation sufficient for its task. Event arrival is not proof that every independently replicated read model has caught up. Prefer an explicit dependency to a blind retry loop that sometimes returns stale absence.

AdvancedCan a serializable database transaction make a cache-plus-remote-payment workflow linearizable?
No. Its guarantee covers participating database operations under its engine contract. The cache and provider need their own authority, observation and recovery protocols.
IntermediateDoes eventual consistency mean any stale answer is acceptable forever?
No. The model and its assumptions still define convergence behavior; the product may additionally require a freshness bound and an operational response when that bound cannot be met.

6. Quorum, partitions and split-brain

A network partition means some participants cannot communicate even though they may still be running. If a rule needs one authoritative order of conflicting writes, isolated groups cannot independently invent that same order without additional coordination assumptions. The system may need to reject/wait for some work or explicitly permit reconcilable conflicts.

A quorum supplies an overlap argument under a membership and protocol model. The inequality R+W>N is not a complete correctness proof: concurrent writers, versions, failures and reconciliation determine what the reader can actually observe. Likewise, electing a replacement leader does not stop an old paused process from acting. Protected resources must reject obsolete authority through an enforced ownership/fencing protocol.

7. What quorum arithmetic proves

With three replicas, choosing write acknowledgements W=2 and read responses R=2 gives W+R>N. Every pair of two-member sets intersects. That is a set-intersection statement. It does not by itself define which version wins, how concurrent writes are ordered, whether acknowledged data survives promotion or whether a read repairs conflicting copies.

StepReplica AReplica BReplica C
Initialx=0x=0x=0
Write v1 acknowledged by A/Bx=v1x=v1x=0
Read B/CNot consultedReturns v1Returns 0
Reader decisionProtocol must choose/resolve a valid resultIntersection supplied one observed copyArithmetic did not define resolution

This is a deliberately simplified thought experiment, not the implementation of a particular database. A real protocol specifies version ordering, membership, failure assumptions and acknowledgement/read semantics. If a read arbitrarily picks the fastest response, an intersecting response containing newer data can still be ignored. If concurrent writes use incompatible clocks, “largest timestamp” can discard an intended update. Product guarantees must come from the complete protocol, not this equation alone.

Membership matters. Two quorums chosen from incompatible configurations can lose the intersection property across a poorly controlled reconfiguration. Consensus-based systems use defined configuration-change and leader/term rules to preserve their guarantees. Do not improvise a new replica list during an incident and assume the old arithmetic still applies.

Partition decision for an invariant-sensitive write:
  Can this side establish the required authoritative ownership/quorum?
    yes -> accept only under that protocol and acknowledgement policy
    no  -> stop/reject/defer protected writes under the contract

Allowing both sides to accept independent stock decrements does not
preserve one global stock invariant unless the domain/protocol already
allocated disjoint rights that make those writes safe.

A timeout is evidence that the caller did not receive a timely answer. It does not distinguish a dead peer from a delayed network, an overloaded process or a committed response that was lost. Promoting another writer merely because one request timed out can leave two actors believing they own the same invariant. Establish ownership and fence stale actors through the supported protocol.

Some domains can operate on preallocated rights during disconnection. For example, separate regions may each sell from a disjoint inventory allocation, with no region exceeding its assigned rights. This changes the model: unused rights elsewhere may be unavailable locally until safely transferred. It is not equivalent to allowing every region to decrement the same unconstrained global counter.

AdvancedWhy not always prefer availability during a partition?
Whether accepting a local write is allowed depends on the invariant. A delayed catalog update may be tolerable, while independently spending the same money is not. State the operation-specific trade-off and any preallocated rights instead of choosing a slogan for the whole system.
IntermediateDoes R+W>N guarantee a single latest value?
Not alone. It provides an intersection under the stated membership assumptions. Version order, conflict handling, concurrent write behavior, durability and reconfiguration semantics still have to establish the claimed read guarantee.

8. Atomic commit versus saga

Prefer one local commit boundary when it naturally owns the invariant. Independent services may instead need atomic coordination supported by their participants, or a workflow allowing visible partial progress. A saga coordinates local steps and recovery actions; it does not turn them into an invisible ACID transaction.

Compensation is a new business action after an earlier commit. A refund may be delayed or fail; a reservation may already be reassigned; an email cannot be unread. Record progress and identities durably, define meaningful recovery for irreversible steps, and do not choose a saga when the required invariant cannot tolerate its intermediate states.

ApproachUse whenTrade-off
One local transactionAll critical data can share an appropriate store/ownerSimpler invariant; less independent ownership.
Two-phase commitParticipants/protocol support atomic commit and its operational cost is acceptableCoordinator/participant failures can block progress in relevant designs.
SagaBusiness permits visible intermediate states and compensationCompensation can fail and is not an automatic rollback.

Refunding a charge is a new financial operation; it may have fees, timing constraints or fail. Releasing a reservation is only valid while its state allows it. Store step identity, status and retry/compensation evidence durably. Avoid a saga for an invariant that the business genuinely requires to be invisible and atomic.

9. Persist a saga decision

A saga coordinates committed local steps, so intermediate business states can be visible. It is not a transaction that later rewinds history. Consider checkout: reserve inventory, authorize payment, then confirm the order. Each participant owns its local invariant and recognizes repeated commands. The orchestrator owns the workflow decision and recovery schedule, not the internal transaction of every participant.

Illustrative durable workflow record:
  saga_id: S7
  order_id: O7
  workflow_version: 12
  phase: PAYMENT_UNKNOWN
  reservation_id: R7
  payment_intent_id: P7
  next_action_id: A19
  next_action: INQUIRE_PAYMENT

Atomic orchestrator transaction:
  compare expected workflow_version and permitted phase
  record the newly accepted participant result
  advance phase/version
  insert next command into local outbox using stable action identity
  commit all of the above

Persist the state transition and next-command intent together. Otherwise a crash can advance the workflow without dispatching the next action, or dispatch an action without remembering why. Participants use action/business identities to return the already established outcome on repetition. A new worker claim or delivery attempt does not create a new payment intention.

Observed participant resultWorkflow decisionWhat must remain unresolved
Inventory hold succeedsStore R7 and request payment P7No paid/confirmed claim yet.
Payment response is lostRecord/payment remains UNKNOWN; inquire original P7Do not infer decline from elapsed HTTP wait.
Payment conclusively failsRelease the specific reservation under an idempotent actionRelease may itself need retry/ownership validation.
Payment succeeds after hold expiresApply explicit reallocation/refund/review policyDo not resurrect a seat/stock unit already sold to another buyer.

Callbacks and polling can race. Use conditional state/version transitions so one accepted observation drives the next action once in the local workflow authority. Record contradictory terminal evidence instead of overwriting it silently. A participant result must be authenticated and bound to the expected order, amount/currency and original action, not accepted because it contains a familiar saga ID.

Contrast two-phase commit. A prepared participant has promised a coordinated outcome and may retain resources while the decision is recovered. It cannot safely treat its own timeout as permission to abort after another participant may have committed. A supported coordinator log and participant recovery protocol are central to that guarantee. A saga avoids that global atomic-commit boundary by accepting intermediate states and explicit compensating business actions, with different isolation and recovery obligations.

An orchestrator is a logical owner, not necessarily one immortal process. Its state must survive restart and its competing workers must obey the workflow version/claim protocol. Choreography moves decisions into participant event reactions; it does not remove the need to identify who notices a stalled workflow, which event permits each transition and how cycles or contradictory events are handled.

AdvancedCan an outbox alone make the saga correct?
No. It makes a local next-action intent recoverable with its state transition. Participants still need safe repetition, valid state transitions, authority checks, unknown-outcome handling and a policy for compensation failures.
IntermediateWhy store workflow_version when commands have unique IDs?
Command identity recognizes repeats. The version/phase predicate protects competing workflow transitions and stale observations. They address different races.

10. Spring and AWS boundaries

Write a durable state machine before selecting an orchestrator. Record the order, current step, stable command/provider identity, state version and unresolved outcome. A conditional transition prevents two workers independently deciding incompatible next states. Recovery must resume from those facts instead of restarting the purchase with fresh identities.

Spring transactions protect configured resources, while an ordinary provider call remains another authority. A database-backed coordinator or a managed workflow can run the protocol, but retry settings do not choose the business recovery rule. Event choreography still needs ownership, deadlines and an inspectable explanation of overall progress. Verify exact consistency behavior for the chosen table, index and regional topology.

Workflow ledger (illustrative):
 orderId, step, commandId, state, version, lastError
 conditional transition: RESERVED -> PAYMENT_PENDING
 remote call: stable payment intent identity
 unknown outcome: reconcile before compensation decision
 outbox: publish only committed local transitions

Spring @Transactional covers the configured local resources. It does not make calls to unrelated payment and stock services atomic. A durable orchestrator can use RDS plus workers, or AWS Step Functions when its state/retry model fits. Choreography uses events instead; it still needs ownership, timeouts and a way to inspect the full workflow.

For DynamoDB, verify the selected consistency mode and exact access path, including secondary indexes and global-table configuration. Never infer multi-region money invariants from a generic product label or from a local strongly consistent read option.

11. Worked design: payment succeeds, stock expires

Follow a reservation racing expiry while payment is in flight. If confirmation wins the authoritative RESERVED transition, expiry must not release that same reservation. If expiry wins, a late payment success cannot simply overwrite the state and promise fulfillment. Resolve it through the declared re-reserve, void or refund policy.

Keep UNKNOWN separate from DECLINED. Before compensation or another irreversible attempt, reconcile the original provider operation. Test duplicated callbacks, stale coordinators, repeated expiry and provider failure during recovery. The customer-visible pending state and operator-owned unresolved tail are part of the design, not exceptional details outside it.

12. Compensate without erasing facts

“Undo the reservation” is incomplete when time and other users have moved on. Release must name the reservation it owns, not increment stock because a workflow failed. If that reservation was already released, repeating the release must not add another unit. If it was confirmed for a completed order, the cancellation policy may require a different transition. Compensation is another stateful business command.

Conceptual reservation-release transaction:
  begin
  transition reservation R from HELD to RELEASED
    only for its authorized tenant/order and expected ownership
  if that transition changed exactly one row:
      return R.quantity to the correct inventory authority
      persist release outcome / next intent
  else:
      resolve existing state; do not increment inventory blindly
  commit

Receipt/action identity and the effect share this local transaction.
The transition predicate must cover every legitimate competing writer.

A refund is likewise a new financial fact linked to the original charge, not deletion of the charge row. Give it a stable refund intention, amount limits and provider recovery identity. A lost refund response can leave the refund UNKNOWN; issuing a second new refund key is not a safe response. Partial refunds require an authoritative remaining-refundable-amount rule under concurrent requests.

EvidenceUnsafe interpretationReconciliation action
Local payment UNKNOWN; provider says successRetry as a new paymentRecord the original success, then evaluate fulfillment/compensation.
Reservation RELEASED; late charge successForce reservation back to HELDApply late-success policy without taking another customer’s stock.
Refund intent exists; provider response missingAssume refund failedInquire the same refund intention; retain unresolved state.
Old restored workflow says payment pendingReplay all steps with new IDsCompare external facts and retained identities before another irreversible action.

Reconciliation needs an eligible population and an owner. Scan durable unresolved intentions by age under a bounded schedule, inquire the original authority and apply conditional state transitions. Preserve the source evidence and classify already resolved, newly resolved, conflicting and still unknown outcomes. A one-off script that makes the pending count zero by overwriting state does not demonstrate recovery.

Bound automatic recovery by supported retention and business deadlines. Once provider lookup or deduplication evidence expires, automation may no longer be able to distinguish no effect from a forgotten effect. Route that limit explicitly to review rather than claiming exactly once. Keep operator actions authorized, scoped and auditable, and preserve the original business identities through manual correction.

A complete failure schedule pauses after every local commit and before every remote acknowledgement. Restart the orchestrator, redeliver callbacks, race expiry against late success and repeat compensation after its own acknowledgement is lost. Verify stock conservation, charge/refund correspondence and exactly the allowed final order states. The target is a defensible final state or a clearly owned unresolved case, not merely a workflow engine status of COMPLETED.

AdvancedMust compensation restore byte-for-byte original database state?
Usually no. Other valid work may have occurred, and charges, notifications or shipments cannot be erased from history. Compensation establishes an allowed new business state while preserving the original facts and respecting current ownership.
IntermediateWhat is an acceptable terminal unresolved state?
Only one explicitly allowed by the business contract, with evidence, an owner, a review/escalation path and visibility to affected users/operators. Naming a state REVIEW_REQUIRED does not by itself resolve the customer obligation.

13. Pitfalls and decision criteria

Stale observations become dangerous when used to authorize mutations. A delayed display can be acceptable while a second charge based on that display is forbidden. Separate the source used for a view from the authority used for the decision and state their freshness explicitly.

When comparing alternatives, identify the invariant owner, coordination participants, availability/latency cost and recovery burden. A named consistency model or workflow product is not evidence that every boundary is protected. Introduce a partition and one crash at each commit point to test the argument.

A compensating action is not guaranteed to restore the exact original world. Emails have been read, stock may have been sold, and refunds can be delayed. Define domain recovery rather than saying “roll back the saga.”

Choose one owner for the invariant when possible. Use replicas/projections for permitted lag. Require atomic coordination only where the contract needs it, and budget the availability/latency cost. Review the design under partitions, not only clean sequential failures.

14. Interview ladder

FoundationalEventual consistency versus immediate visibility?
Eventual convergence permits temporary differences; immediate visibility/read-your-writes requires a stronger selected path or protocol.
FoundationalIs compensation a rollback?
No. It is a new domain action intended to offset an earlier effect and can itself fail or be incomplete.
IntermediateDoes R+W>N prove linearizability?
No. It describes overlap under assumptions; versions, concurrent writes, membership and failure/reconciliation semantics determine the real guarantee.
IntermediateCan @Transactional span arbitrary microservices?
Not automatically. It covers configured transactional resources; remote business calls need an explicit coordination/recovery protocol.
AdvancedWhen is a saga inappropriate?
When required invariants cannot tolerate visible intermediate states or meaningful compensation is impossible, unless the contract is changed or a different coordination boundary is chosen.
AdvancedHow do you handle late payment success after expiry?
Use conditional durable states, reconcile provider identity, and follow a documented re-reserve/void/refund policy. Never infer outcome solely from timeout or overwrite a newer state.
IntermediateDoes a fresh read make checkout across two services atomic?
No. Freshness constrains an observation, while atomic commitment constrains a set of changes. Identify the authorities and commit protocol; independent local commits require explicit intermediate states and recovery.
AdvancedWhy can payment succeed after a reservation expires?
The remote operation and expiry have independent timing/commit boundaries. Conditional reservation transitions establish the winning local state; reconcile the original payment identity and apply the declared re-reserve, void or refund policy.
