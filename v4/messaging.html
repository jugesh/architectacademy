Library/Messaging & Pipelines/Messaging

Messaging · fundamentals to production judgment

Move work asynchronously and own every delivery boundary

Begin with a slow receipt email in checkout. Learn queues and event logs, acknowledgements, duplicates, ordering, outbox/inbox patterns, and operational recovery.

Technical definitionAsynchronous messaging transfers commands or events through an intermediary so production and consumption need not complete in the same request. Delivery guarantees describe a particular boundary; they do not automatically describe the business effect.
Before you begin. Understand the local transaction boundary and the uncertainty after an HTTP timeout. A broker is simply the service holding messages between producer and consumer.

Learning outcome: Design a durable producer-to-consumer flow and explain what happens after a crash before or after acknowledgement.

1. Remove email from the checkout wait

Checkout already committed the order, but a slow email provider is keeping the HTTP caller waiting. The business can often acknowledge the order before the receipt is sent. Separate those completion points explicitly: order acceptance is one durable fact; sending the receipt is pending work with its own owner and deadline.

An in-memory executor changes timing but does not preserve work after a process crash. A durable handoff records the intent independently of the current request thread. This removes one lifetime dependency and introduces new responsibilities: recognizing repeated delivery, measuring delayed completion and deciding when the intermediary may stop retaining work.

2. Identify exactly what an acknowledgement proves

Read this slowly: it establishes the distinction used in the implementation sections.

An asynchronous message represents work or a fact that another process can handle later. Delivery means the broker made it available to a consumer. Processing means the consumer attempted its handler. Completion means the intended business effect reached its defined durable boundary. An acknowledgement tells the broker when it may stop redelivering; it is not automatically evidence that a remote shipment or payment completed.

Commit an order and an outbox row together. A relay publishes the row, then records publication. If it crashes between those steps, it may publish again. A consumer can record the event ID and its local mutation together so a repeat does not apply that mutation twice. If it calls an external carrier, a second boundary remains: the carrier may act before the consumer records success. Carry stable provider identity and reconcile that uncertainty.

Check your understanding
FoundationalExactly-once delivery eliminates duplicate business effects everywhere.
A guarantee has a scope. Broker transactions do not automatically include an external database or provider. Identify which resources participate, how retries are recognized, and what evidence survives a crash.
IntermediateWhen should a consumer acknowledge?
After the durable work required by its processing contract commits, with explicit recovery for remote effects. Acknowledging before durable handoff risks loss; acknowledging after a crash can lead to redelivery, which must be safe.

3. Follow one operation before adding patterns

Carry this concrete operation through the later mechanisms.

If a Java thread sends the receipt after the HTTP response, a process crash can lose the thread and its pending work. If a queue holds the task, another worker can receive it after the first worker dies. But the queue does not know whether the email provider already acted. Persisting pending work solves one failure window and exposes the next. Keep producer commit, broker delivery, consumer commit and external effect separate throughout the lesson.

4. Define acceptance and completion separately

Name the business acceptance point and the final effect. “No lost receipt intent after order commit” requires a durable connection between the local change and publication; “no duplicate shipment” requires protection at the effect boundary. These are separate guarantees. A broker feature solving delivery does not automatically solve either local atomicity or remote-provider uncertainty.

A command requests an action from a responsible owner; an event describes a fact that interested consumers can interpret. SendReceipt is a command, while OrderAccepted is an event. Define their identity, schema, ownership, retry horizon and completion expectations. Independent consumers need independent progress and failure handling so analytics cannot accidentally govern shipping availability.

Functional requirements

  • Accept an order and schedule receipt work.
  • Allow fulfillment and analytics to react independently.
  • Retry transient delivery errors and recover failed work.

Targets and invariants

  • No lost receipt intent after order commit.
  • Duplicate delivery must not duplicate protected business effects.
  • Illustrative oldest receipt age under two minutes.
  • One broken message must not block all healthy work.

A command asks someone to act, such as SendReceipt. An event records a fact, such as OrderAccepted. Events often have several independent consumers. Commands should have clear ownership; events should carry enough identity and contract information for consumers to interpret them safely.

5. Queue versus retained event log

We know why pending work must survive. Choose how independent readers share or replay it.

A work queue assigns pending work among competing workers. Publish/subscribe creates separate delivery paths for independent reactions. A retained log keeps records by policy while consumers track positions. Choose from the reader’s required behavior: one owner processing a job, several independent reactions, or consumers needing ordered replay while history remains available.

In a Kafka consumer group, partition assignment constrains parallel progress; adding more consumers than useful partition work does not force additional throughput. Different groups can read independently. In SQS, distinguish standard-queue repeat/ordering behavior from FIFO ordering and send deduplication. In both cases, the application must define when a durable business effect is complete.

Ordering also has scope. Grouping by order ID can serialize that order’s events without imposing one global sequence on every order. A hot order/key or a slow handler can still constrain the chosen scope. Preserve the business reason for ordering rather than assuming a globally ordered stream is always safer.

ModelReader behaviorUse
Work queueWorkers compete for tasks; successful processing removes/acknowledges workJobs and independently retried commands.
Publish/subscribeEach subscription gets its own delivery pathSeveral independent reactions to an event.
Partitioned logConsumers track positions; records are retained by policyReplay, ordered per-partition streams, projections.

In Kafka, a consumer group normally divides partition ownership among its members; different groups can independently consume the same retained records. Parallelism within one group is constrained by partitions and assignment. In SQS standard queues, consumers compete and delivery can repeat; ordering is best-effort. FIFO adds specific ordering/deduplication semantics, not automatic atomicity with your database.

6. Walk through the acknowledgement window

The broker can deliver work; next establish what proof lets it stop redelivering.

Follow one delivery through receive, local commit and acknowledgement. If the consumer acknowledges first and then dies, the effect can be lost. If it commits first and dies before acknowledging, the message can repeat. Those failure windows are unavoidable when the broker and local store do not share the relevant atomic protocol; the processing contract must tolerate the selected recovery behavior.

For a protected local mutation, insert a consumer-scoped event receipt and apply the mutation in one transaction. A unique identity decides whether that work already committed. If it did, the repeat can acknowledge without repeating the effect. If the transaction failed, the receipt must not remain as false proof of completion. Different legitimate consumers need different receipt scopes.

A remote carrier or payment provider remains outside that local transaction. The inbox can prove that a local fulfillment task was created once; it cannot by itself prove one remote shipment. Persist the remote intent, reuse its provider identity and reconcile unknown outcomes. Retain receipts for the actual redelivery/replay horizon, not an arbitrary cleanup interval.

Safe local-effect pattern:
BEGIN;
INSERT INTO inbox(consumer, event_id) VALUES (...)
  ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING;
-- If already present, skip the effect and acknowledge.
-- Otherwise apply local domain mutation in this transaction.
COMMIT;
ACK message / commit consumed position afterward;

The inbox uniqueness constraint and local mutation must share the same commit. A remote shipment provider remains outside that transaction; it needs its own retry identity/status lookup or reconciliation. Retain deduplication records long enough for the replay/redelivery horizon. Deleting them early permits old events to act again.

7. Commit the consumer effect

Now let fulfillment consume OrderPaid event E. The local effect is to make an order ready for packing. We need one committed local effect per consumer/event identity even if E is delivered repeatedly. The consumer name matters: fulfillment and analytics must each be allowed to process the same event. Do not globally mark E consumed for every subscriber.

CREATE TABLE consumer_receipt (
  consumer_name text NOT NULL,
  event_id uuid NOT NULL,
  payload_hash text NOT NULL,
  completed_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
  PRIMARY KEY (consumer_name, event_id)
);

-- Consumer transaction, AFTER schema and authorization validation:
BEGIN;
INSERT INTO consumer_receipt (consumer_name, event_id, payload_hash)
VALUES ('fulfillment-v1', :event, :hash)
ON CONFLICT (consumer_name, event_id) DO NOTHING
RETURNING event_id;
-- Duplicate branch: compare stored hash; do not repeat the effect.
-- New branch only:
UPDATE fulfillment_order
SET state = 'READY', version = :event_version
WHERE tenant_id = :tenant AND order_id = :order
  AND state = 'AWAITING_PAYMENT'
  AND version = :expected_previous_version;
-- Require the domain-approved result. A gap/conflict may need rollback.
-- Insert any next external-work intent into this database's outbox.
COMMIT;
-- Only after this commit, advance the broker acknowledgement/offset.

This is a protocol outline: the fulfillment table and event-version contract belong to the domain model. A version predicate is useful only when producers and consumers agree what the version counts. If the consumer receives only selected event types, source aggregate versions can skip legitimately; “previous + 1” would then reject valid events. Define either a complete per-aggregate stream, a dedicated consumer sequence or a transition rule that can safely skip irrelevant source changes.

If the new-event update cannot apply, do not commit a completed receipt merely because an INSERT succeeded. Roll back and classify the conflict: missing prerequisite, stale event, invalid transition or a real incompatible schema. A business-idempotent transition may deliberately regard a known already-completed state as success, but establish that it belongs to the same business intention. “It is already READY” is not enough if the event refers to a different payment or order version.

The receipt is evidence of completed local work because it commits in the same transaction as that work. A preliminary receipt committed separately breaks that meaning. Its payload hash also detects illegal reuse of one event identity for changed content. Define a canonical event representation for the comparison, and quarantine a mismatch instead of overwriting the old receipt.

Sending a shipment request or email inside this transaction does not extend database atomicity to the remote service. Persist a shipment intent, then use a stable provider request identity and reconciliation. If the remote service cannot deduplicate or answer an inquiry, expose the weaker guarantee. A correct local inbox is not proof of exactly-once real-world shipment.

If a blocked event must be parked to allow progress, atomically store the complete recoverable event and its PARKED state before advancing the source. The recovery handler needs a distinct state transition from PARKED to APPLIED; a generic duplicate check that treats every stored receipt as completed would strand it forever. Monitor parked age and give it an owner.

IntermediateCan I keep consumed IDs in Redis while effects are committed in PostgreSQL?
Only if the resulting cross-store failure protocol meets the requirement. A cache eviction or a crash between the independent writes can permit repeats or lose work. A receipt in the same local transaction is easier to reason about for this database effect.

8. Close the producer dual-write gap

The producer has its own dual-write problem. Database-first can commit an order and crash before publishing; broker-first can publish a fact for an order that never commits. Record the business change and publication intent in the same local transaction. The relay reads committed intents and publishes them later, so a producer crash leaves recoverable pending work.

The relay still has a publish/mark-sent gap. If publication succeeded and the relay dies before recording that result, it can publish again. Keep event identity stable across those attempts. The outbox changes an unrecorded omission into recoverable potentially repeated delivery; the consumer’s effect protocol handles repetition.

An after-commit callback can reduce perceived delay but is not a substitute for durable pending intent. Similarly, committing an SQL transaction and a broker transaction in sequence does not erase the failure interval between them. State the exact participating resources and recovery ledger instead of implying an annotation supplies universal atomicity.

@Transactional
public Order accept(CreateOrder cmd) {
  Order order = orders.insert(cmd);
  outbox.insert(new Event(order.id(), "OrderAccepted", 1));
  return order;
}
// Relay publishes committed rows; publish success and "sent" marking can race.
// Expect duplicate publication and keep eventId stable.

Spring transaction management can cover the local database work. A Spring Kafka or AWS SDK producer sends from the relay. Broker transactions have useful scopes, but do not assume an annotation atomically commits PostgreSQL, Kafka, SQS and a provider. Verify the exact transaction boundary for the chosen client/infrastructure.

9. Build an outbox relay

An outbox makes the need to publish durable alongside the business mutation. It does not make publication and the later “sent” marker atomic across a database and broker. Follow event E: the checkout transaction commits a reservation and E in the same database. A relay discovers E, sends it, waits for the configured broker acknowledgement, and then records publication. If the process dies after the broker accepted E but before the marker commits, the next relay sends E again. Duplication is the expected recovery path, not an exceptional violation of the outbox pattern.

-- Illustrative PostgreSQL outbox; same transaction as business writes.
CREATE TABLE order_outbox (
  event_id uuid PRIMARY KEY,
  tenant_id uuid NOT NULL,
  aggregate_id uuid NOT NULL,
  aggregate_version bigint NOT NULL,
  event_type text NOT NULL,
  payload jsonb NOT NULL,
  created_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
  published_at timestamptz,
  lease_until timestamptz,
  claim_epoch bigint NOT NULL DEFAULT 0
);
CREATE INDEX outbox_pending ON order_outbox (created_at, event_id)
WHERE published_at IS NULL;

Claim a bounded batch in a short transaction, release database locks, then perform network I/O. The following is an unordered, lease-based dispatcher. It is suitable only where consumers can tolerate/detect reordering or another ordering protocol is added. Simply sorting claims does not guarantee per-order publication order when several relay workers run.

BEGIN;
WITH candidates AS (
  SELECT event_id FROM order_outbox
  WHERE published_at IS NULL
    AND (lease_until IS NULL OR lease_until < now())
  ORDER BY created_at, event_id
  FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED
  LIMIT 50
)
UPDATE order_outbox o
SET lease_until = now() + interval '30 seconds',
    claim_epoch = o.claim_epoch + 1
FROM candidates c
WHERE o.event_id = c.event_id
RETURNING o.*;
COMMIT;
-- Send the returned events OUTSIDE this database transaction.
-- On a confirmed broker acknowledgement for the claimed epoch:
UPDATE order_outbox SET published_at = now(), lease_until = NULL
WHERE event_id = :event AND claim_epoch = :claimed_epoch
  AND published_at IS NULL;

Thirty seconds and fifty rows are illustrative, not recommended defaults. Size the lease and batch from publish duration, process pauses and renewal behavior. A recovered worker increments claim_epoch. The old worker cannot mark a newer claim as published, but it may still send to the broker after its lease expired. Database fencing protects this database update; it does not retroactively cancel a network send. Consumers must still recognize E by its durable event identity.

WindowRiskProtocol response
Commit business/outbox failsNo accepted business state or intentTransaction rolls back; original command may retry.

Store the event’s business representation or a deliberately versioned reconstruction contract. If a relay rereads a mutable order after the fact, it may publish today’s state as yesterday’s event. For strict per-aggregate sequence, choose a compatible approach such as ordered source-log capture or serialized dispatch of an aggregate’s next event, and test it through takeover. A Kafka partition preserves the order actually appended to it; it does not repair two relay workers sending version 8 before version 7.

Operate from oldest unpublished age, claim failures, broker acknowledgement latency and repeated publication, not only pending count. Cleanup must respect replay/audit needs and downstream retention. Deleting a row because a send method returned before its acknowledgement future completed can permanently lose the intent.

AdvancedDoes claim_epoch make the relay exactly once?
No. It prevents a stale worker from completing a newer database claim under this condition. A stale or uncertain send can still reach the broker. Stable event identity and a safe consumer protocol handle the duplicate effect.
  • PostgreSQL SELECT: locking clauses and SKIP LOCKED behavior.

10. Ordering, schema evolution, and replay

Arrival order, business event order and effect completion order can differ. One handler may finish slowly while a later event’s asynchronous effect finishes first. Include an aggregate identity and a version/ordering rule where the business requires one. A delayed old state should not blindly overwrite a newer state merely because it arrived later.

Schema compatibility must cover retained history and delayed work, not only the newest producer and consumer. Define how readers treat optional additions, unknown event types and incompatible versions. A poison event may require quarantine, but skipping it can let later events for the same aggregate overtake a prerequisite. Choose that ordering/recovery policy explicitly.

Replay is safe only for the effects being rebuilt. Reconstructing a search projection can use a replay-specific path that suppresses external email or shipment. Keep stable source identities, define the starting snapshot/log position and verify completeness. Resetting an offset alone does not prove a correct reconstruction or restore history that retention already removed.

11. Offsets and ownership

A Kafka offset is a position in one partition, not a global business sequence. For a consumer group, committing offset 252 declares that the next record to resume from is 252. If record 250 is still being processed while 251 completed, committing 252 skips unfinished 250 after a crash. Parallel handlers need a contiguous completion frontier for each partition; the highest finished offset is not sufficient.

Partition 3 delivery:
  offset 250 -> slow database transaction, NOT committed yet
  offset 251 -> local transaction committed

Safe committed next offset: 250
Unsafe committed next offset: 252

After 250 also commits, contiguous frontier becomes 252.
If the process crashes at frontier 250, 250 and 251 may replay.
The local receipt handles the already-committed 251 repeat.

This example assumes every record below the frontier was either applied durably or handed to a defined durable recovery path. The commit mechanism, worker handoff and group lifecycle must agree on that meaning. Keep the Kafka consumer’s ownership/commit operations under the client’s supported threading model. Offloading work does not remove poll interval, buffer size and rebalance obligations.

When ownership moves, stop handing new work to the revoked partition, fence or drain the old tasks according to the framework contract, and commit only what is safely completed while ownership permits it. A paused JVM can resume an old task after another consumer acquired the partition. Broker ownership helps delivery coordination; a conditional database transition and receipt still defend the effect against stale or overlapping work.

Partition key selection connects throughput and order. Keying by order ID brings that order’s events to one partition under the chosen routing, while unrelated orders can progress elsewhere. One very active order/key still has a sequential bottleneck. Increasing partition count can change key-to-partition mapping for future records, so a migration requiring strict key order must manage old and new histories rather than assuming automatic continuity.

For SQS, extend visibility for legitimate long work under a bounded policy, and plan for extension failure or a stalled worker. A very long visibility timeout delays recovery after a crash; a short one raises concurrent redelivery risk. Neither replaces repeat-safe processing. For Kafka, a retained log permits intentional replay only while the required records remain available; retention is part of the recovery contract.

AdvancedCan Kafka exactly-once processing make the database transaction and a topic output atomic?
Not by itself for an arbitrary external database. Kafka’s transaction protocol covers its participating records/offsets. Use an explicitly supported connector/protocol or a database outbox/inbox design, and state precisely which boundary has the guarantee.
AdvancedShould I commit a poison record so the partition can continue?
Only after an allowed terminal decision or durable recovery handoff has been recorded. Skipping without retaining enough evidence sacrifices the business work. Whether later same-key events may proceed is a domain ordering decision.
  • Kafka design: partition positions, delivery semantics and transactional scope.
  • SQS visibility: delivery visibility, extensions and failure recovery.

12. SQS and Kafka operational choices

Acknowledge/delete and commit-position APIs expose broker-specific progress. In SQS, visibility temporarily hides received work; expiration can make it available to another worker. In Kafka, consumer positions and group participation have their own poll/processing rules. Neither replaces effect idempotency. Configure the actual Spring listener/client behavior rather than assuming return from a method always means the same acknowledgement boundary.

Use bounded retries for transient failures and a deliberate path for deterministic failures. Quarantine preserves the event, error, schema and business identity for diagnosis. A dead-letter queue is a location for unresolved work, not resolution. Fix the reader or data under policy, replay with original identities and prove the business state recovered.

Measure age and useful completions alongside broker counters. A decreasing queue can mean successful processing, expiration, quarantine or loss under a bad acceptance contract. Scale consumers only within database/provider capacity and ordering constraints. Backlog per worker is useful only when its relationship to processing cost and the completion target is understood.

ProgressMessage acknowledgement/deletion and visibilityConsumer position/offset by partition.
Slow consumerVisibility can expire; extend appropriatelyPoll/processing and group timeouts need correct tuning.
RetryRedelivery, delayed queues, DLQ policyRetry topics or controlled retry; ordering trade-offs.
ReplayExplicit retained source/archive strategyReset positions while records remain retained.

SQS visibility timeout is a lease-like hiding period, not exclusive ownership forever. A second worker may receive the message when it expires. Bound processing time, extend visibility when appropriate, and keep effects idempotent anyway. Kafka lag counts how far a consumer is behind; also measure oldest-event age and actual processing failures.

A poison message fails repeatedly because of its data or a deterministic bug. After a bounded policy, quarantine it with diagnostics and ownership. A DLQ is not resolution: operators need a fix, replay authorization, and proof the replay cannot duplicate earlier effects.

13. Worked design: fulfillment after checkout

Use the outbox and inbox together, then identify the remote warehouse boundary they do not cover.

Commit the confirmed order transition and OrderPaid intent locally. A relay publishes the stable event. A fulfillment consumer commits its receipt and a local fulfillment task together, then acknowledges. A separate runner performs the remote warehouse interaction using a durable fulfillment identity. Each boundary has a ledger that survives the current process.

Now lose the warehouse response after acceptance. The local task becomes UNKNOWN until provider lookup, supported repeat-safe retry or reconciliation establishes the result. Creating a fresh shipment because the worker timed out would discard the identity needed to prevent duplication. Analytics and receipt delivery use independent progress so their failures cannot consume fulfillment’s entire budget.

Validate every gap: order commit/before publication, publish/before sent mark, local consumer commit/before ack, and remote acceptance/before result recording. Include duplicate, delayed, out-of-order and incompatible-schema deliveries. Assert durable fulfillment identity and state, while monitoring oldest business-state age and unresolved recovery work.

14. Backpressure and recovery

A queue absorbs a burst only when the system can later process the admitted work within its deadline. Sustained excess arrivals produce backlog age and cost, even if HTTP acceptance stays fast. Bound admissions or provide a declared deferred/rejected response before accepting work the system cannot honor.

“Exactly once” requires a scope. Supported Kafka transactions can coordinate selected Kafka writes and consumed positions under the configured model; they do not make an arbitrary external SQL, email or provider effect atomic. Keep the resource boundary, duplicate identity and recovery protocol visible in every design claim.

“Exactly once” always needs a scope. Kafka transactional processing can atomically coordinate supported Kafka operations/positions under the configured model; it does not make an arbitrary email or SQL mutation exactly once. State the boundary and prove the effect separately.

15. Backlog recovery budget

A queue trades immediate coupling for a recovery obligation. Estimate how quickly it can clear accepted work while new work continues. Use comparable units: completed business jobs per second, not polls or delivery attempts. If a job produces three downstream calls, the provider budget must include that fan-out and all retries.

Illustrative steady recovery:
  existing backlog B = 120,000 jobs
  new arrivals lambda = 600 jobs/s
  sustainable useful completion mu = 800 jobs/s
  net drain = mu - lambda = 200 jobs/s
  time to clear = B / (mu - lambda) = 600 s = 10 min

If arrivals rise to 900 jobs/s at the same capacity:
  backlog grows by 100 jobs/s; it never drains in steady state.
If redelivery doubles handler attempts but useful mu stays 800:
  attempt throughput is not a doubling of recovery capacity.

The ten-minute estimate assumes a stationary mix, no new failure and enough capacity for each partition, database and downstream dependency. A hot ordered key can violate the fleet-average assumption. A few permanently failing jobs can also dominate the oldest-age signal while most work is current. Report age and count by bounded workload class, plus the size and age of parked/unresolved work.

Set recovery targets from the business deadline and retention. If the oldest job has already spent 20 minutes waiting and the completion deadline is 25 minutes, a ten-minute drain estimate is insufficient for that cohort. Decide whether to prioritize urgent eligible work, acquire validated capacity, degrade optional work or explicitly expire an operation. Expiry must define the business outcome; dropping an accepted paid order is not just queue housekeeping.

IncidentUnsafe reactionControlled response
Database slowsDouble all consumers immediatelyReduce/hold admitted concurrency; inspect locks and useful completion.
Poison payload repeatsRetry forever with no ownerClassify and preserve it through a durable quarantine policy.

A dead-letter queue is a place to retain failed deliveries, not a business resolution. Record why the item moved, what parser/schema failed and what the recovery operator may safely do. Managed broker redrive and a custom publish-then-delete routine have different guarantees. In a custom routine, a crash between those independent operations can duplicate or lose the intended handoff unless the protocol accounts for the window.

Before a replay, define the source range, consumer version, original event identities, expected duplicate behavior, ordering impact and stop criteria. Dry-run comparisons can expose incompatible old schemas, but a dry run cannot prove remote side effects are safe. Begin with a bounded cohort, observe useful completions and invariants, then expand. Keep a reconciliation report of applied, already applied, rejected and still unresolved work.

AdvancedWhich graph proves the incident is over: queue depth falling or consumer error rate zero?
Neither alone. Verify timely useful completion, correct final business state, bounded unresolved age and safe resource use. A queue can shrink because messages expired or were discarded, while errors become zero because consumers stopped.
IntermediateWhy can adding workers reduce completion throughput?
More concurrent attempts can increase lock contention, connection waits, retries and provider throttling. Measure the operating point of the constrained dependency and bound the fleet’s aggregate pressure.

16. Interview ladder

FoundationalWhy use a queue?
To decouple timing, persist pending work and buffer bursts. It introduces delayed completion, consumer operations and delivery semantics.
FoundationalCommand versus event?
A command asks for an action and has an owner; an event describes a fact that can have independent consumers.
IntermediateWhy acknowledge after commit?
Acknowledging first risks losing work if the commit never happens. Commit first can cause redelivery, so make the effect idempotent through a shared local transaction or another appropriate protocol.
IntermediateWhat does an outbox guarantee?
The local business change and pending event are committed together. Publication can repeat, so consumers still need safe duplicate handling.
AdvancedHow do you rebuild a projection safely?
Use retained events/snapshot plus a consistent change position, handle versions, isolate rebuild traffic and suppress external business effects. Verify completeness against the source.
AdvancedWhat if FIFO messages still create duplicates?
Send deduplication, visibility and redelivery are different boundaries. Protect the consumer effect with stable business identity, conditional transitions and appropriate retention.
IntermediateWhy does an outbox still require consumer deduplication?
The relay can publish successfully and crash before recording publication. Republishing is the safe recovery for that uncertainty. A stable event identity plus the consumer’s atomic receipt/effect boundary handles the repeat.
AdvancedCan a dead-letter queue prove the business recovered?
No. It isolates unresolved work. Preserve identity, establish failure cause and ordering impact, repair under policy, replay safely and verify the intended durable business state. Queue depth alone cannot establish completion.
