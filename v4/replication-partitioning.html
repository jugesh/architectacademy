Replication & Partitioning · fundamentals to production judgment

Copy data for resilience, divide data for capacity

Follow a growing order store through read replicas, failover, sharding keys, hot partitions and safe rebalancing.

Technical definitionReplication maintains copies of data across nodes. Partitioning divides a dataset or workload into separately managed subsets. These mechanisms can be combined, but they address different capacity and failure questions.
Before you begin. Read Databases and consistency guarantees. We begin with one writer and one replica.

Learning outcome: Choose keys and replication policy, identify failover data-loss risks, and describe a rebalancing protocol.

1. The order database reaches two different limits

The database lesson introduced additional copies for reads and partitions for distribution. Now make ownership explicit. A replica maintains a copy of logical data; a partition owns a subset of data/work. Neither mechanism removes the need to coordinate conflicting decisions or recover from an old owner returning.

Follow an order write, a replica read and a failover separately. The write’s acknowledgement policy determines known durability; replication mode affects what a reader may see; failover chooses who may accept later writes. A product offering “replication” does not answer all three questions without its configuration and protocol.

2. Ask whether you need another copy or another owner

Read this slowly: it establishes the distinction used in the implementation sections.

Replication makes copies of a logical dataset. Partitioning assigns subsets to different storage or execution owners. Copies can improve failure tolerance or read distribution; partitions can distribute capacity. Both introduce coordination. The routing key determines locality, while the distribution of real keys determines whether load is balanced.

Partition orders by tenant. Most queries stay local, but one merchant generates half the traffic. Adding partitions for other tenants does little for that merchant. Splitting it by time or an additional bucket distributes work while making some reads fan out. During rebalancing, establish which owner may accept writes and how old owners are fenced. Routing changes without ownership control can create conflicting writers.

Check your understanding
FoundationalA replacement leader is safe because the old one is unreachable.
Unreachable to the coordinator does not mean stopped. The old process may still reach storage or clients. Use an ownership protocol and fencing token or storage mechanism that rejects obsolete authority.
IntermediateWhy can a good average partition load hide an incident?
One hot key can dominate tail latency while most partitions are idle. Measure skew, busiest partitions and per-key classes, then choose a split that the workload can actually use.

3. List routing and recovery guarantees

Define read freshness, acknowledged-write survival and recovery-time expectations per operation. A dashboard can tolerate lag that is unacceptable for a new order lookup. A low data-loss objective may impose coordination or replication costs that differ from a best-effort browse view.

For partitioning, record query locality, cross-key invariants, data size and real key popularity. Tenant count alone does not predict load balance. Plan rebalancing and failure capacity before the dataset or workload outgrows one owner.

Functional requirements

  • Route order lookup and recent-order queries.
  • Continue or recover after writer loss.
  • Move data when partitions grow.

Targets and invariants

  • Define RPO for acknowledged writes.
  • Read-your-writes for critical user paths.
  • Prevent stale writers after promotion.
  • Avoid hotspots and preserve tenant isolation during movement.

Measure data volume, key-frequency distribution and access locality. A partition containing 10% of rows can receive 90% of requests. Equal row counts do not imply balanced load.

4. Follow a write to its replicas

In asynchronous replication, the writer can acknowledge before a follower applies the change. Routing a fresh post-write lookup to that follower can produce stale absence. Synchronous acknowledgement can strengthen selected durability/visibility properties but increases coordination and needs a precise failure policy.

During failover, distinguish choosing a new leader from preventing obsolete writes. The old process may be paused or isolated rather than dead. Terms, epochs or fencing must be enforced at the protected resource. A coordinator’s lease expiry is not a physical stop signal to code still running elsewhere.

Synchronous replication waits for specified replica acknowledgement before returning under the configured policy. It can reduce certain loss windows at a latency/availability cost. Asynchronous replication improves decoupling but can leave a lagging promotion candidate missing acknowledged writes. Exact durable/apply guarantees depend on the engine and settings.

Failover must establish one authority, update routing and handle in-flight clients. Fencing prevents the old writer from continuing protected writes after recovery. A successful DNS change does not instantly replace every pooled connection.

5. Derive a partition key

A partition key determines where work is routed. A tenant key makes many tenant queries local, yet one large tenant may dominate a partition. Splitting by time or another bucket can distribute work while requiring fan-out, aggregation or additional projections. Evaluate the busiest keys, not just average partition utilization.

A single coordinated inventory claim may remain hot regardless of partition count. Splitting its authority changes the business protocol, such as allocating bounded stock to separate owners. Consistent hashing can reduce movement under membership changes, but does not by itself solve skew, replication, write ownership or the semantics of concurrent rebalancing.

Choose access paths and transaction locality together. A globally unique external order ID may require a coordinating index/owner if uniqueness spans shards. Do not assume each shard’s local unique constraint enforces a global rule.

Consistent hashing reduces key movement when membership changes relative to a simple modulo mapping under common assumptions. Virtual nodes can smooth assignment; replication placement, weighted capacity and hot keys still require design. The hashing algorithm does not move data safely by itself.

6. Rebalance with explicit ownership

Rebalancing moves ownership while traffic continues. Establish a source baseline plus subsequent changes, route reads/writes under a controlled protocol and fence obsolete writers at cutover. A copied dataset that looks complete at one instant can still lose updates made during transfer.

Verify critical identities, versions and invariants, and define rollback after new-owner writes begin. Pointing clients back is insufficient if the old owner lacks committed target facts. Budget the load of copy, catch-up and repair so movement does not overload the system it is meant to expand.

Conceptual shard move:
  record migration epoch and source owner
  copy a consistent dataset / record change position
  catch up writes while source remains authoritative
  verify checksums/business counts and lag
  switch ownership with fencing and routing epoch
  retain rollback evidence; retire source only after policy permits

Dual writes can diverge if either destination fails. Prefer a tested change-capture/replication protocol with an authoritative writer, or define conflict handling and reconciliation explicitly. During cutover, a stale router must not commit to the old owner. A forwarding layer may help, but it needs epoch and retry semantics.

Spring Data does not choose a safe shard key automatically. RDS read replicas and engine partitioning have distinct capabilities. DynamoDB partition-key design distributes access under service behavior; verify key/index limits and adaptive behavior rather than assuming capacity is uniform for any key.

7. Worked design: one oversized merchant

Partition order history by a key matching its access pattern and keep the live inventory authority separate where its invariant requires coordination. Use replicas only for operations whose freshness contract permits the selected path. Track hot partitions and lag alongside aggregate throughput.

Exercise leader loss, a paused old leader returning, lagging reads and migration during writes. Confirm that acknowledged facts survive under the declared policy and obsolete authority cannot commit protected effects. A successful routing change is not proof of data reconciliation or safe write ownership.

Work the boundary through a failure

Consider a client that receives a committed order version 18 from the write authority and immediately reads through a lagging replica at version 17. The older read does not reverse the write; it violates read-your-writes if that session was promised it. One possible protocol carries a minimum observed version and routes/waits until a replica can satisfy it, with a bounded fallback to the authority. This requires a real comparable version/freshness mechanism, not an arbitrary wall-clock delay.

Now lose the writer. Before promoting another replica, establish the supported fencing and durability protocol: which acknowledged facts can be absent, and how is the old writer prevented from continuing? Promotion alone cannot create missing committed history or revoke an isolated writer’s authority. If the offered replication mode permits an acknowledged-write loss window, expose it as part of the recovery guarantee and reconcile related external effects.

8. Failure cases to rehearse

Redundancy is not backup: an accidental delete or corrupt write can replicate successfully. Retain independent recoverable history and rehearse restore/reconciliation. Also test failure-domain concentration; copies sharing one dependency may fail together.

Large aggregate capacity can hide a hot key, lagging follower or exhausted failover target. Make ownership, skew and lag observable. More shards or replicas are useful only when they address the measured operation without weakening its required guarantees.

CaseRequired reasoning
Promote a lagging replicaQuantify possible lost writes and reconcile acknowledged intents.
Old primary returnsPrevent split authority with fencing/epoch policy.
Hot key after adding shardsA single key is not split by more partitions alone.
Global query across shardsBudget fan-out, partial failure and pagination/ordering.

Track per-shard latency, traffic, storage, replication lag and rebalancing progress. A fleet average can hide one overloaded shard. Backups remain essential because corruption/deletion can replicate correctly.

9. Interview ladder

FoundationalReplication versus partitioning?
Replication copies; partitioning divides. A partition can itself have replicas for availability.
FoundationalDoes a read replica scale all writes?
No. It can serve suitable reads, while the primary write path may remain the bottleneck.
IntermediateWhy is tenant ID sometimes a poor key?
It gives locality but can concentrate a very large tenant on one partition. Evaluate actual size and traffic skew.
IntermediateWhat does consistent hashing not solve?
It does not guarantee hot-key balance, transaction locality, replica placement or correct migration/fencing.
AdvancedHow do you prevent stale writes during a shard move?
Use an authoritative ownership/epoch protocol enforced at the write boundary, then copy/catch up/verify and switch with defined retries.
AdvancedHow do you enforce uniqueness across shards?
Assign a suitable global owner/index or coordination protocol, or redefine the uniqueness scope. Independent local constraints do not enforce a global rule.
IntermediateWhy does another replica not automatically fix stale post-create reads?
The selected replica may not have applied the acknowledged write. Define read-your-writes and route/use a protocol that supplies it for that journey; do not infer freshness from replica count.
AdvancedWhat prevents an old leader from writing after failover?
An enforced ownership/fencing protocol at the protected resource, using terms/epochs or equivalent authority checks. Election or lease expiry elsewhere does not physically stop a paused process from returning.
