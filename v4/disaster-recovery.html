Disaster Recovery · fundamentals to production judgment

Prove you can recover the business state, not just restart servers

Derive backup, failover, reconciliation and failback choices from acceptable downtime and data loss.

Technical definitionDisaster recovery restores required service and data after a major failure. Recovery time objective (RTO) is the acceptable recovery duration; recovery point objective (RPO) is the acceptable data-loss window, each defined for a workload and scenario.
Before you begin. Read replication and failover and Reliability. A replica and a backup solve different failure cases.

Learning outcome: Write a measurable recovery protocol with verified data restoration, ownership, reconciliation and return-to-normal steps.

1. The replica copied the accidental delete

An operator deletes orders with an incorrect predicate. The standby faithfully replicates the delete. Promoting it does not recover the lost records. High availability kept copies current; this incident needs a historical recovery point and a way to reconcile later valid changes.

A regional failure is different again: data may exist elsewhere, but networking, credentials, quotas and dependent providers may not be ready. Start with scenarios and business consequences. “We have backups” is an inventory statement, not a measured recovery capability.

2. Define recovery in business terms

Read this slowly: it establishes the distinction used in the implementation sections.

A backup is a recoverable representation of data at some point. RPO is the tolerated data-loss window; RTO is the tolerated recovery duration. A replica can copy accidental corruption, so redundancy and recovery are distinct. A successful restore must recover a usable business state, not merely a running database.

Restore an order database to 10:00 after an incident at 10:05. A payment completed at 10:03 in an external provider may now have no matching local order transition. Before resuming charges, compare provider identities, outbox/inbox state and ledger records. Decide the authoritative source for each fact and prevent replay from issuing a second effect. Recovery includes the reconciliation interval.

Check your understanding
FoundationalAn available secondary region means regional recovery is proven.
Failover still depends on routing, secrets, capacity, replication lag and write ownership. Failback introduces another transition. Measure the entire rehearsal rather than counting deployed resources.
IntermediateWhat should a restore drill prove?
Usable data, application compatibility, dependency access, observed data-loss window, recovery time and business reconciliation. Preserve evidence and repeat after significant topology or schema changes.

3. Agree on recovery objectives

Functional requirements

  • Restore the order/payment ledger after corruption or regional loss.
  • Reconcile external provider outcomes and accepted messages.
  • Return safely to the primary operating model.

Targets and invariants

  • Example ledger RPO: five minutes; RTO: one hour, validated by drills.
  • No dual active authority for protected writes.
  • Recovery artifacts and keys available during the scenario.
  • Audit evidence and explicit incident ownership.

Those objectives are illustrative. A payment ledger may need stricter loss handling than a rebuildable search index. Inventory each data class, dependency and contractual obligation rather than imposing one target on the whole estate.

4. Select a recovery posture

PostureWhat exists before disasterTrade-off
Backup/restoreBackups and reproducible infrastructureLower steady cost; slower recovery and restore work.
Pilot lightEssential data/core components readyMore startup and orchestration at incident time.
Warm standbyReduced running environmentFaster recovery; scaling/quotas still need proof.
Active/activeMultiple serving environmentsData ownership, conflicts and split authority become harder.

Choose from measured recovery duration, cost and correctness, not prestige. Replication lag influences RPO; routing, scaling, validation and operator decisions influence RTO. Active/active does not eliminate recovery from bad writes or compromised credentials.

5. Back up enough to reconstruct a valid system

Capture data, schema, relevant configuration, event position and required encryption-key access. Point-in-time recovery can restore before an incident when supported, but valid writes after that point may need replay/reconciliation. Test retained event/schema compatibility and provider references.

Keep protection against accidental deletion and inappropriate access with suitable isolation/immutability/retention controls. Do not copy secrets into a general backup artifact without policy. Verify restoration using an isolated environment and meaningful consistency/business checks; a checksum only proves bytes match a reference, not that the reference is business-correct.

6. AWS and Spring recovery boundaries

RDS backup/PITR and snapshot features, S3 versioning/replication and AWS Backup can support different recovery plans. Verify retention, cross-account/region availability, encryption keys and chosen service topology. Infrastructure as code rebuilds configuration; it does not reconstruct missing business data by itself.

Recovery checklist (workload-specific):
 incident scope + write-stop/authority decision
 restore target and time/source position
 schema/config/key availability
 ledger invariants + provider reconciliation
 routes, credentials, quota and capacity validation
 controlled reopen + monitored failback plan

Spring services restarting against a recovered database must tolerate schema/state and replayed messages. Reconcile inbox/outbox positions: a restored ledger may have forgotten an effect a provider already performed. Stable external identity is crucial here; replaying everything as new commands can multiply damage.

7. Worked drill: recover checkout after regional failure

Declare a regional scenario and start the RTO clock at the agreed point. Establish whether the old writer is fenced/unreachable and choose the secondary data point. Quantify replication loss against the required RPO before reopening writes. Validate order totals, pending payment intents, outbox backlog and provider connectivity.

Restore routing and compute with bounded worker drain. Reconcile payments accepted externally after the secondary’s data point using original provider references/settlement evidence. If history is incomplete, quarantine uncertain work for controlled investigation rather than assuming absence means failure.

When the original region returns, do not allow it to write stale state. Resynchronize or rebuild it from the current authority, validate, then conduct a separately planned failback. Record measured data loss, recovery time, operator actions and deviations for the next drill.

8. Recovery mistakes to avoid

MistakeConsequence
Untested backupRestore/key/schema dependencies fail under pressure.
Failover without fencingTwo writers can commit conflicting protected state.
Forget third-party effectsRestored local state can retry already-completed payments.
Failback as DNS flipOld data and pooled connections can reintroduce stale authority.

Run drills with realistic data size, not an empty database. Measure restore throughput, replay time, DNS/connection behavior and quota approvals. A successful exercise must prove business invariants and the return path, not only that a web page responds.

9. Interview ladder

FoundationalRTO versus RPO?
RTO bounds acceptable recovery duration; RPO bounds acceptable data-loss time window for the specified scenario/workload.
FoundationalWhy is a replica not a backup?
It may quickly copy deletes/corruption. Historical recovery needs retained points and a tested restore protocol.
IntermediateWhat must be restored besides rows?
Schema, configuration, access to keys/secrets, event positions, dependencies and enough evidence to reconcile external effects.
IntermediateDoes active/active remove DR?
No. Bad writes, compromise and cross-region dependencies can affect every active copy; recovery and ownership still require design.
AdvancedHow can restoring create duplicate charges?
The restored ledger may lack a completed external effect. Preserve/query provider identity and reconcile before retrying unknown work.
AdvancedWhat proves a DR plan works?
A realistic timed drill demonstrates the declared RPO/RTO, invariants, dependency readiness, controlled authority switch and tested failback.
