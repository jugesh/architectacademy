Kafka Consumer Groups and Rebalancing

Partitions, offset ownership, delivery guarantees, rebalance failure modes and operational tuning.

Partitioned log model Consumer groups and capacity What a rebalance does At-least-once and duplicates Slow processing and poison messages

1. Partitioned log model

Where is ordering guaranteed?

A Kafka topic consists of partitions, each an ordered append-only log. Ordering is guaranteed within a partition, not across a topic. A record key commonly determines partition assignment; choose keys based on the required ordering domain and skew risk. More partitions can increase parallelism but also metadata, file handles and coordination overhead.

2. Consumer groups and capacity

Why do extra consumers sit idle?

Within a consumer group, each partition is assigned to at most one member at a time. With 12 partitions and 20 consumers, at most 12 consumers can actively process assigned partitions. Separate groups consume independently. The broker stores records; the group tracks progress through committed offsets. An offset is a position, not proof of successful downstream processing.

3. What a rebalance does

Why does scaling sometimes reduce throughput temporarily?

A membership or partition change requires reassignment. Eager rebalancing revokes broad assignments, interrupting processing. Cooperative rebalancing can move partitions incrementally when supported by the chosen assignor and clients. Static membership can reduce avoidable churn for stable deployments. Always verify actual client and broker versions; behavior varies with group protocol.

4. At-least-once and duplicates

What happens between DB commit and offset commit?

Commit offsets only after the business side effect is durable. If the effect succeeds but the consumer crashes before offset commit, the record is processed again. Commit before the effect risks message loss. Make the business operation idempotent using a unique event ID or aggregate version. Kafka transactions can coordinate Kafka writes and offsets, but not arbitrary external database transactions.

5. Slow processing and poison messages

How do you isolate a poison message?

Long processing can exceed max.poll.interval.ms, causing group membership changes. Separate polling from bounded processing carefully, manage pause/resume and preserve per-partition ordering. Use retries with backoff and a dead-letter strategy that explicitly documents ordering tradeoffs. Monitor consumer lag, processing age, rebalance frequency and downstream errors; lag alone cannot identify root cause.

Interview and design review

Try answering before expanding.
Do consumer groups provide exactly-once database writes?

No. Offset commits and external DB commits are not one atomic transaction. Use deduplication and business constraints, or design a transactional outbox/inbox boundary.

Why is partition count an upper bound on active consumers?

A partition has only one assigned consumer per group at a time to preserve sequential consumption within that partition.

What triggers a rebalance?

Membership changes, timeouts and partition topology changes can trigger reassignment. Client protocol and assignment strategy determine disruption.

How would you debug increasing lag?

Compare production rate to completed processing rate, partition skew, slow downstream calls, processing failures, rebalances and consumer resource saturation.

Partitioning arithmetic and skew

Why can lag grow with spare consumers?

For a topic with 24 partitions and a group of 8 healthy consumers, the assignment typically averages 3 partitions each. Increasing the group to 30 cannot create more than 24 active partition owners. Yet 24 partitions do not guarantee even load: if one customer key produces 45% of records, its single partition may dominate lag and tail processing time. Repartitioning changes key mapping and can affect ordering assumptions; splitting a hot aggregate across partitions sacrifices per-aggregate order unless the application reconstructs it. Scale based on per-partition processing rate and lag age, not only total consumer count.

Why can lag grow with spare consumers?

A single hot partition can bottleneck one consumer while other consumers are idle; partition ownership is exclusive within the group.

Exactly-once boundary and consumer recovery

Trace the invariant, failure boundary and recovery.

Kafka partition ordering applies only within one partition, not across a topic. Committing an offset after writing to SQL creates at-least-once effects: a crash after SQL commit but before offset commit replays the message. Make the SQL transaction include both the business update and a unique processed-event marker; on replay, the marker prevents duplicate business effects. Commit the Kafka offset only after the database transaction succeeds. Kafka transactions can coordinate Kafka read-process-write flows, but do not automatically include an external SQL database. Handle poison records with bounded retries, a DLQ policy and explicit operational replay. Track oldest-message age, per-partition lag, processing time, rebalance count and hot partitions.

Design review and interview follow-ups

Why does Kafka exactly-once not guarantee one SQL update?

Kafka transactional guarantees apply to supported Kafka operations; SQL side effects require their own atomic deduplication or coordinated transaction design.

How do you preserve order while scaling?

Choose a partition key matching the ordering domain, then scale partitions and consumers within that constraint. Hot keys can cap parallelism.

Producer delivery and ordering

Does acks=all guarantee no data loss?

A Kafka producer batches records and retries transient failures. Idempotent producer settings reduce duplicates caused by retries within supported producer semantics, while application-level logical duplicates remain possible. Ordering is per partition and depends on key choice and producer behavior. Acks, replication factor and minimum in-sync replicas jointly affect durability and availability; acks=all alone is not a complete durability guarantee. Test broker failures and leadership changes with the actual topic configuration.

Does acks=all guarantee no data loss?

Not by itself. Replication health, min.insync.replicas, unclean leader election and operational failure assumptions matter.

Consumer offset and rebalance state machine

Why does a consumer appear healthy but stop processing?

Consumers poll, process, then commit offsets according to their chosen delivery contract. Committing before business effects risks loss; committing after effects risks replay. A rebalance revokes and reassigns partitions, so long-running processing must respect max.poll.interval.ms and partition ownership. Cooperative rebalancing can reduce disruption but does not eliminate it. Make handlers restartable, ensure idempotent effects and distinguish record lag from time lag.

Why does a consumer appear healthy but stop processing?

It may be blocked on a dependency, exceed poll intervals, repeatedly rebalance or be stuck retrying a poison record.

Schema evolution and replay strategy

What must be checked before replaying a topic?

Event schemas are contracts. Add optional fields and maintain backward-compatible readers before producers emit new variants; compatibility details depend on serialization format and registry policy. A replay may trigger emails, payments or external API calls again unless those side effects are idempotent or explicitly disabled. Define retention, compaction, dead-letter ownership, replay authorization and recovery runbooks. Consumer group offsets are not a substitute for durable business audit state.

What must be checked before replaying a topic?

Consumer side effects, schema compatibility, deduplication keys, ordering assumptions and downstream capacity.

Kafka offsets, failures and replay lab

The atomicity problem

A consumer reads Kafka, updates PostgreSQL and commits its Kafka offset. Kafka offset commit and SQL commit are separate effects. If SQL commits and the consumer crashes before offset commit, Kafka redelivers the record. Design for duplicate processing rather than assuming the broker can magically coordinate the SQL transaction.

Kafka topic partition -> consumer -> SQL transaction:
                                     1. INSERT processed_event(id)
                                     2. update business state
                                     3. COMMIT
                              then -> commit Kafka offset

Deduplicate in the same SQL transaction

CREATE TABLE processed_event (
  consumer_name varchar(80) NOT NULL,
  event_id      varchar(120) NOT NULL,
  processed_at  timestamp NOT NULL,
  PRIMARY KEY (consumer_name, event_id)
);

Within one database transaction, attempt to insert the marker and apply the business mutation. On a duplicate-key conflict, roll back the failed statement/transaction as appropriate for the database, then treat the event as already processed; do not blindly continue a transaction left in error state. The identifier must be globally stable for the logical event within the consumer's deduplication scope.

Broker and consumer failure windows

  1. Crash before SQL commit: SQL rolls back, Kafka replay applies once.
  2. Crash after SQL commit before offset commit: replay detects processed_event and skips mutation.
  3. Commit Kafka offset before SQL effect: message can be permanently missed; avoid this ordering.
  4. Handler fails repeatedly on one record: apply bounded retry and DLQ strategy; account for ordering requirements.
  5. Consumer revocation during a long handler: ensure no unsafe processing by a stale partition owner.

Partitions and keys

Kafka guarantees ordering within a partition, not total ordering across a topic. If all events for account 42 must be ordered, use that account ID as partition key and design around hot accounts. With 24 partitions, no more than 24 consumers in a single group can actively own those partitions at once. A hot partition is not fixed by adding consumers to the group: you may need key-level redesign while preserving ordering.

Producer durability

Idempotent producer configuration reduces retry duplicates under Kafka's supported semantics; it does not remove duplicates introduced by upstream business workflows. Replication factor, minimum in-sync replicas, acknowledgments and leader-election policy jointly affect loss and availability. Test these settings under broker termination, partition leader changes and network faults.

Replays are operationally hazardous

Replaying an order event can resend confirmation emails or repeat provider calls unless effects are deduplicated. Replay runbooks should define scope, starting offsets, dry-run mode, downstream rate limits, side-effect suppression when appropriate and post-replay reconciliation. Schema evolution needs backward-compatible consumers and clear event versioning.

Incident questions

Does Kafka exactly-once processing solve SQL deduplication? Kafka transactions cover supported Kafka flows, not automatically arbitrary SQL transactions; protect SQL effects separately.

What if a record has no stable event ID? Introduce one at production or derive a sufficiently stable, documented identity; partition-offset identity is tied to log position rather than business intent.

Why can committed consumer lag be low but work incomplete? Offsets may advance before durable effect, or async processing may still be outstanding. Measure business completion separately.

What if one key produces 60% of traffic? One partition may dominate. Repartitioning by finer key increases parallelism but can break per-key ordering expectations.

How should DLQs be operated? With alerting, ownership, retention, replay policy, cause classification, and safeguards against poison-message loops.

Kafka operations: Senior Interview and Design Review

Answer before reading. Every question pairs a technical explanation with a verification exercise or architectural follow-up.

1. Why does Kafka offset commit after SQL still allow duplicates?

Explain the trade-off and test.

The SQL transaction may commit and the consumer can crash before Kafka offset commit. The broker redelivers, so business effects must be idempotent with an event ID or another domain invariant. Offset management controls replay position, not external transactional atomicity.

Follow-up / exercise: Diagram the crash between SQL commit and offset commit.

2. Why is committing offset before SQL dangerous?

Explain the trade-off and test.

If the offset advances and the SQL transaction then fails, the record can be skipped on restart, causing data loss at the business level. Commit the offset only after the durable effect, accepting duplicates and suppressing them using database uniqueness.

Follow-up / exercise: Demonstrate with a consumer killed at both possible boundaries.

3. How do you implement SQL deduplication?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Insert (consumer,event_id) into a table with a unique constraint in the same transaction as the domain update. On unique conflict, roll back or handle the database-specific statement failure correctly and skip the repeated effect. Use stable event identity across producer retries and replay.

Follow-up / exercise: Explain why an in-memory processed set cannot survive restart.

4. What does exactly-once mean in Kafka?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Kafka's supported idempotent producer and transactional mechanisms can provide specific broker/log processing guarantees, especially for Kafka-to-Kafka flows. These guarantees do not atomically include an arbitrary payment API or SQL database. Write down the boundary before claiming exactly once.

Follow-up / exercise: Ask for the exact transaction participants and durability assumptions.

5. How does partition count constrain consumers?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Within one consumer group, a partition is owned by one active consumer at a time, so 24 partitions cap useful active ownership at 24 consumers for that topic. More consumers may be idle. Hot partitions can dominate lag even when most consumers are free.

Follow-up / exercise: Discuss a single account key generating half the traffic.

6. What ordering does Kafka guarantee?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Records in a partition have a defined append order. There is no intrinsic total order across partitions, and producer retries or multi-key business workflows complicate application expectations. Choose a key representing the ordering domain and define reordering tolerance for cross-key events.

Follow-up / exercise: Can a consumer assume all events in a topic are globally ordered?

7. What causes repeated rebalances?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Group membership changes, crashes, long processing exceeding poll interval or configuration changes can trigger assignment changes. Rebalances can disrupt progress and increase lag; cooperative strategies can reduce revocation scope but not remove correctness requirements.

Follow-up / exercise: Inspect max.poll.interval.ms and processing-time distribution before tuning session timeout.

8. How should poison messages be treated?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Differentiate transient dependency failure from malformed or semantically invalid records. Apply bounded retry with backoff and a DLQ policy; for strict per-key ordering, skipping a record can violate later events' assumptions. DLQ ownership and controlled replay are essential.

Follow-up / exercise: What does the application do while one record blocks a partition?

9. What is consumer lag versus message age?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Lag counts unconsumed offsets while age measures how old pending records are; a low lag can still hide high business processing delay if offsets are acknowledged early or async handlers lag. Instrument durable completion and oldest-unprocessed age.

Follow-up / exercise: Explain why lag zero does not prove all customer emails were sent.

10. What does acks=all guarantee?

Explain the trade-off and test.

The producer waits for acknowledgments from in-sync replicas according to topic/broker configuration, but durability depends on min.insync.replicas, replica health and failure policies. It is not proof against every possible correlated failure or operational misconfiguration.

Follow-up / exercise: Compare RF=3, minISR=2 and an unavailable replica.

11. Why does replay require change control?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Consumers may trigger payments, alerts or emails again; historical schemas may be incompatible and downstream systems can be overloaded. Define offset ranges, deduplication strategy, throttles, effect suppression, reconciliation and rollback before replay.

Follow-up / exercise: Replay one day's events without duplicating external notifications.

12. When would you choose a queue rather than Kafka?

Explain the trade-off and test.

A work queue may suit independent tasks with straightforward acknowledgments, visibility timeouts and per-message retries. Kafka suits durable partitioned event streams with multiple consumer groups and log replay. Choose based on fan-out, ordering, retention, replay and operational complexity rather than popularity.

Follow-up / exercise: Contrast SQS-like processing with audit stream requirements.
