ANSWERED ADVANCED INTERVIEW BANK · 12 SCENARIOS

Kafka operations: Senior Interview and Design Review

Answer before reading. Every question pairs a technical explanation with a verification exercise or architectural follow-up.

1. Why does Kafka offset commit after SQL still allow duplicates?

Explain the trade-off and test.

The SQL transaction may commit and the consumer can crash before Kafka offset commit. The broker redelivers, so business effects must be idempotent with an event ID or another domain invariant. Offset management controls replay position, not external transactional atomicity.

Follow-up / exercise: Diagram the crash between SQL commit and offset commit.

2. Why is committing offset before SQL dangerous?

Explain the trade-off and test.

If the offset advances and the SQL transaction then fails, the record can be skipped on restart, causing data loss at the business level. Commit the offset only after the durable effect, accepting duplicates and suppressing them using database uniqueness.

Follow-up / exercise: Demonstrate with a consumer killed at both possible boundaries.

3. How do you implement SQL deduplication?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Insert (consumer,event_id) into a table with a unique constraint in the same transaction as the domain update. On unique conflict, roll back or handle the database-specific statement failure correctly and skip the repeated effect. Use stable event identity across producer retries and replay.

Follow-up / exercise: Explain why an in-memory processed set cannot survive restart.

4. What does exactly-once mean in Kafka?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Kafka's supported idempotent producer and transactional mechanisms can provide specific broker/log processing guarantees, especially for Kafka-to-Kafka flows. These guarantees do not atomically include an arbitrary payment API or SQL database. Write down the boundary before claiming exactly once.

Follow-up / exercise: Ask for the exact transaction participants and durability assumptions.

5. How does partition count constrain consumers?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Within one consumer group, a partition is owned by one active consumer at a time, so 24 partitions cap useful active ownership at 24 consumers for that topic. More consumers may be idle. Hot partitions can dominate lag even when most consumers are free.

Follow-up / exercise: Discuss a single account key generating half the traffic.

6. What ordering does Kafka guarantee?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Records in a partition have a defined append order. There is no intrinsic total order across partitions, and producer retries or multi-key business workflows complicate application expectations. Choose a key representing the ordering domain and define reordering tolerance for cross-key events.

Follow-up / exercise: Can a consumer assume all events in a topic are globally ordered?

7. What causes repeated rebalances?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Group membership changes, crashes, long processing exceeding poll interval or configuration changes can trigger assignment changes. Rebalances can disrupt progress and increase lag; cooperative strategies can reduce revocation scope but not remove correctness requirements.

Follow-up / exercise: Inspect max.poll.interval.ms and processing-time distribution before tuning session timeout.

8. How should poison messages be treated?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Differentiate transient dependency failure from malformed or semantically invalid records. Apply bounded retry with backoff and a DLQ policy; for strict per-key ordering, skipping a record can violate later events' assumptions. DLQ ownership and controlled replay are essential.

Follow-up / exercise: What does the application do while one record blocks a partition?

9. What is consumer lag versus message age?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Lag counts unconsumed offsets while age measures how old pending records are; a low lag can still hide high business processing delay if offsets are acknowledged early or async handlers lag. Instrument durable completion and oldest-unprocessed age.

Follow-up / exercise: Explain why lag zero does not prove all customer emails were sent.

10. What does acks=all guarantee?

Explain the trade-off and test.

The producer waits for acknowledgments from in-sync replicas according to topic/broker configuration, but durability depends on min.insync.replicas, replica health and failure policies. It is not proof against every possible correlated failure or operational misconfiguration.

Follow-up / exercise: Compare RF=3, minISR=2 and an unavailable replica.

11. Why does replay require change control?

Explain the trade-off and test.

Consumers may trigger payments, alerts or emails again; historical schemas may be incompatible and downstream systems can be overloaded. Define offset ranges, deduplication strategy, throttles, effect suppression, reconciliation and rollback before replay.

Follow-up / exercise: Replay one day's events without duplicating external notifications.

12. When would you choose a queue rather than Kafka?

Explain the trade-off and test.

A work queue may suit independent tasks with straightforward acknowledgments, visibility timeouts and per-message retries. Kafka suits durable partitioned event streams with multiple consumer groups and log replay. Choose based on fan-out, ordering, retention, replay and operational complexity rather than popularity.

Follow-up / exercise: Contrast SQS-like processing with audit stream requirements.
